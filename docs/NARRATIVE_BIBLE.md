# Narrative Bible

## Working title

**The Last Covenant: An Isekai Necromancer Chronicle**

## Premise

A person from modern Earth awakens in the freshly executed body of Vael Corven, a condemned necromancer. Their heart does not beat. Their memories remain, but fragments of Vael's life surface at moments of danger. A second voice lives behind those fragments.

The Radiant Church declares the resurrection the fulfillment of an apocalyptic omen. The unquiet dead recognize an old covenant and call the protagonist by titles they have never earned.

## Central thematic question

Who has authority over the dead: gods, institutions, descendants, the dead themselves, or the person with the power to act?

Supporting themes:

- identity in a borrowed body;
- consent after death;
- memory as evidence and weapon;
- whether continuity makes a person;
- institutions that do good through a hidden atrocity;
- responsibility created by power;
- grief versus possession;
- survival versus dignity.

## Hidden world truth

Death is not functioning naturally. The gods and their institutions redirect departing souls through the Luminous Gate, extracting memory and identity as divine sustenance before returning fragments to the cycle. The system once protected reality from something older beneath existence, but centuries of exploitation have destabilized it.

Necromancy was outlawed not simply because it violates the dead. Skilled necromancers can perceive the extraction, interrupt it, and preserve a complete soul. Some necromantic practices are genuine atrocities; others threaten divine political control.

This truth must be revealed gradually and remain open to interpretation. The Church contains sincere protectors, opportunists, reformers, and people unaware of the system.

## Tone

- gothic, intimate, mysterious, and politically tense;
- serious consequences with moments of warmth, dry humor, and wonder;
- horror through implication, identity, and moral pressure rather than constant gore;
- characters speak from personal histories, not as lore dispensers;
- the protagonist is powerful but never omniscient.

Avoid parody isekai conventions, gratuitous cruelty, empty edginess, and a simple religion-is-evil conclusion.

## The protagonist

The player controls the Earth-born mind inside Vael's body. Character creation defines Earth origin, values, instincts, and the earliest response to the body.

Open questions driving Act I:

- Why did the transfer occur?
- Is Vael dead, fragmented, or the second voice?
- Why does the body recognize rituals the player never learned?
- Who altered the execution?
- What was the original Last Covenant?

Character knowledge must be tracked separately from facts visible to the content engine.

## Metaphysics

A person has:

- **Vessel:** material body and embodied habits.
- **Breath:** living animating force.
- **Name:** continuity recognized by self and others.
- **Memory:** experiences, including fragmentary echoes in body and place.
- **Will:** capacity for choice.
- **Thread:** connection to the wider cycle.

Different necromancy schools manipulate different layers. A body moving does not prove the original person is present.

## Major factions

### The Radiant Church

Public purpose: protect the living, guide the dead, suppress predatory magic.

Internal divisions:

- Lanterns: healers and local clergy;
- Inquisition of the Final Dawn: dangerous-magic hunters;
- Keepers of the Gate: secret custodians of soul extraction;
- Reformist Choir: suspects corruption in the passage rites.

Act I face: Inquisitor Seraphine Vale.

### The Hollow Court

A clandestine polity of persistent dead seeking recognition and territory.

Internal conflict:

- preservationists want safe secrecy;
- sovereignists want a nation;
- revanchists want rule over the living;
- the Unnamed fear that preserving identity forever causes another catastrophe.

Act I face: Morrow, a speaking skull claiming to be a dead king.

### The Ashen Empire

An expansionist state that industrializes necromancy while publicly condemning uncontrolled practitioners. It uses legally erased prisoners and debtors after death.

Its argument: undead labor prevents famine and living casualties. Its practice: personhood is defined away for efficiency.

Act I face: Legate Cassian Rook.

### The Shepherds

A decentralized tradition that helps spirits resolve attachments and pass beyond known divine channels. They distrust permanent undeath and coercion but disagree on whether preserved souls should remain.

Act I face: Mother Sable.

### The Nameless Below

Not a conventional faction. It may be a pre-divine intelligence, the collective remainder of stripped souls, or the absence against which personhood defines itself. Never confirm one interpretation too early.

## MVP region: Vhal March

A border region around the settlement of **Dunmire**, the **Grieving Wood**, and the sealed necropolis **Saint Orra's Descent**.

Dunmire survives through pilgrimage, gravekeeping, and trade. Recent dead are speaking during funerals. Graves are emptying without signs of excavation. The Church has quarantined the old cemetery.

Regional pressures:

- crop blight;
- refugees from imperial expansion;
- missing children who report dreams of the necropolis;
- contested burial rights;
- rumors of Vael's return.

## Core companions

### Seraphine Vale

An inquisitor sworn to destroy Vael. Disciplined, compassionate in practice, uncompromising about coercive necromancy. Her dead brother's soul was damaged by official rites.

Arc question: Can an institution be reformed when obedience shaped your identity?

Boundaries: objects strongly to soul enslavement and desecration; may accept consensual return under extraordinary evidence.

### Morrow

A witty speaking skull claiming to be King Morcant, founder of the Hollow Court. His memories contain contradictions and may be assembled from several people.

Arc question: If a community recognizes a constructed identity, is it less real?

Boundaries: fears erasure more than pain; manipulates others to preserve himself.

### Lys

A plague-marked adolescent who sees probable deaths as dates and images. Her power may be sensitivity to damaged Threads rather than prophecy.

Arc question: Is foreknowledge a duty to intervene or permission to live without control?

Boundaries: refuses to be treated as a tool; responds strongly to the player's treatment of powerless spirits.

### Sir Aldren Marr

A knight who voluntarily entered undeath to keep an oath protecting Dunmire. His covenant is failing, and each battle erodes a memory.

Arc question: Does duty remain noble when it consumes the person who chose it?

Boundaries: obeys legitimate defense orders but refuses atrocities even at the cost of dissolution.

## Antagonistic forces

- High Keeper Elian Voss, who believes controlled soul sacrifice prevents cosmic collapse;
- the remnants of Vael's former circle;
- an imperial acquisition team seeking the protagonist's vessel;
- the protagonist's second voice;
- player-created enemies produced by broken promises.

Antagonists need coherent goals, useful truths, and lines they will not cross.

## Act I structure

### Prologue — The Body on the Gallows

Awakening, escape or confrontation, first contact with a spirit, and discovery that the protagonist's heartbeat is absent. Origin-specific solutions establish tone.

### Chapter 1 — The Dead of Dunmire

Enter the settlement openly, disguised, or through the cemetery. Investigate speaking corpses. Meet Seraphine, Lys, and Aldren. First resurrection forces a method choice: coercion, covenant, echo, or release.

### Chapter 2 — Names in the Wood

The Grieving Wood contains memory phenomena and missing villagers. Morrow is found or awakened. Evidence suggests funeral rites are stripping more than grief.

### Chapter 3 — Saint Orra's Descent

Competing factions enter the necropolis. Player choices determine companions, public reputation, and access route. Vael's body recognizes the seal.

### Chapter 4 — The First Covenant

Reveal that Saint Orra negotiated an earlier arrangement between the living, dead, and Gate. The protagonist chooses to restore, alter, expose, exploit, or temporarily contain it.

## Act I outcome families

- **Merciful Custodian:** spirits are stabilized or released with consent, but the Church marks the protagonist as a theological threat.
- **Hollow Sovereign:** the dead gain protected standing, escalating political conflict with the living.
- **Necessary Tyrant:** the crisis is controlled through binding and secrecy, saving lives while creating future rebellion.
- Variants depend on companions, faction debts, witnesses, destroyed evidence, and the first named undead.

No outcome is the canonical good ending.

## Narrative state categories

- world facts;
- character knowledge;
- player-facing discoveries;
- rumors and their sources;
- promises and debts;
- witnessed methods;
- lies told and believed;
- companion private state;
- irreversible losses;
- unresolved mysteries.

## Writing rules

- Present sensory evidence before explanation.
- Choices should describe intent, not falsely guarantee outcome.
- Do not hide information the character should clearly know.
- Do not reveal mechanical percentages unless the selected mode permits it.
- Track pronouns, titles, names, injuries, and current bodies.
- Reactions reference witnessed or credibly transmitted actions.
- Every named dead character has at least one prior relationship or unfinished concern.
- Humor comes from character and contrast, not undermining grief.
- Lore entries are spoiler-safe and update as interpretations change.

## Content safety

The game includes death, grief, body alteration, coercion, religious abuse, identity loss, and moral horror. Provide granular content notes and options to reduce vivid body-horror description. Never use sexual violence as ambient worldbuilding or a shortcut for villainy.
