# Endings and Epilogue Engine

## Philosophy

Endings are deterministic authored compositions. The game evaluates what the player built, destroyed, promised, and became. It does not ask the player to choose an ending label.

## Ending state model

Primary dimensions:

- cycle model;
- soul ownership;
- memory policy;
- undead legal status;
- divine status;
- barrier mechanism;
- enforcement authority;
- protagonist identity/body;
- faction governments;
- companion outcomes;
- regional survival;
- unresolved debts and promises.

## Ending families

1. **Restored Cycle** — repair the old system with reforms or renewed secrecy.
2. **Free Passage** — souls choose departure without divine extraction; barrier cost moves elsewhere.
3. **Common Covenant** — distributed institutions maintain death through shared contribution.
4. **Kingdom Eternal** — the dead become a permanent sovereign civilization.
5. **Necromancer Ascendant** — protagonist personally enforces the order.
6. **New Pantheon** — gods are replaced, reconstituted, or democratized.
7. **World Without Death** — continuity becomes default, creating population and identity crises.
8. **Merciful Oblivion** — identity ends cleanly and the Below loses purchase.
9. **Open Door** — the division between life and death is dissolved.
10. **The Necessary Lie** — stability is preserved by hiding the truth.
11. **Broken Covenant** — no stable agreement; regions develop conflicting death systems.
12. **Nameless World** — catastrophic or intentionally post-identity existence.

Each family has successful, compromised, unstable, and tragic variants.

## Epilogue composition order

1. cosmic result;
2. protagonist;
3. companions;
4. major factions;
5. regions;
6. named undead and promises;
7. long-term historical interpretation;
8. New Game Plus hooks.

Specific modules override generic ones. The compiler rejects incompatible modules without precedence.

## Companion epilogues

Companion endings depend on personal arc state, not only global ending. A companion may oppose the final covenant, leave before it, enforce it, become its first victim, or survive outside it.

## Failure endings

Hard failure requires clear causality and prior warning. Examples:

- all continuity anchors destroyed;
- covenant enforcement promised to incompatible authorities;
- the Below accepted without identity protection;
- protagonist loses self while no successor can act;
- barrier collapses after all replacement mechanisms are eliminated.

Provide a final checkpoint before irreversible endgame sequences.

## Ending transparency

After completion, an optional Chronicle explains major decisions and consequences without exposing every hidden variable. It may show newly unlocked routes for replay.

## Testing

Ending tests require:

- clause compatibility matrix;
- at least one reachable fixture per family;
- contradiction detection;
- epilogue precedence tests;
- companion independence;
- migration compatibility;
- golden checksums for representative campaigns.
