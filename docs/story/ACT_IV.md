# Act IV — The Hunger of Heaven

## Purpose

Act IV reveals the metaphysical machinery without reducing previous conflicts to a simple conspiracy. The gods are culpable and necessary under the current system. The player must decide what truth, identity, and cosmic risk mean.

## Entry pressure

The cycle is failing visibly:

- newborns speak with inherited memories;
- funerals no longer complete;
- empty vessels awaken;
- divine miracles become unstable;
- erased names form hostile phenomena;
- regions experience overlapping histories.

Act III outcomes determine who controls access, resources, and public belief.

## Realms

- the Pilgrim Stair;
- Archive of Unlived Lives;
- Choir of Gatekeepers;
- divine dominions shaped by consumed memories;
- the Luminous Gate mechanism;
- Vael's divided interior landscape;
- the shore of the Nameless Below.

## Revealed history

The original gods discovered that complete souls passing beyond the world weakened a barrier. They began consuming memories to reinforce it. Later gods inherited the system and gradually converted necessity into privilege.

Saint Orra created a local covenant proving willing memory contributions could work. Vael attempted a global replacement using distributed consent and preserved identity. To prevent divine seizure, he split key knowledge across his body, soul, enemies, and an identity outside the world's contracts: the Earth-born protagonist.

## Identity resolution

The Second Voice may be:

- Vael's surviving will;
- a defensive simulation created from body memory;
- a shard of the Below wearing Vael's habits;
- a composite shaped by player actions.

Evidence supports multiple readings. The final state can be separation, integration, restoration, exile, imprisonment, mutual destruction, or acknowledged coexistence.

## Major chapters

### IV.1 — Miracles in Decay

Resolve crises showing both the value and harm of divine systems.

### IV.2 — The Heresy Made Public

Choose evidence, audience, timing, and interpretation. Truth can reform or shatter societies.

### IV.3 — Gods with Human Memories

Encounter divine entities whose personalities are built partly from consumed dead.

### IV.4 — Trial of the Outsider

The world's contracts cannot classify the protagonist. Factions attempt recognition, ownership, banishment, or elevation.

### IV.5 — Vael's Divided House

Explore memory architecture and resolve protagonist/Vael/Second Voice identity.

### IV.6 — Open the Gate

Enter the mechanism and recover final covenant clauses while the barrier weakens.

## Church outcomes

- transparent reformation;
- schism;
- militant denial;
- collapse;
- secular funerary network;
- player-controlled theology;
- decentralized Lantern tradition;
- divine restoration.

## Companion culmination

Each companion must make an independent choice. The player can influence eligibility and trust but cannot directly select the companion's conclusion.

## Act IV completion

The player leaves with the complete covenant grammar, an identity configuration, knowledge of the true cost, and a coalition—or empire—capable of reaching the final convergence.
