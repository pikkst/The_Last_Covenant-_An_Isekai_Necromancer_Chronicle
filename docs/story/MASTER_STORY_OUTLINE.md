# Master Story Outline

## Campaign promise

The complete campaign follows an Earth-born protagonist inside the executed necromancer Vael Corven's body as they uncover why death is failing, decide whether the dead are property or people, and finally rewrite—or preserve—the covenant between mortals, gods, and the force beneath death.

The story spans five acts. Each act changes the scale without abandoning personal consequences:

1. one body and one settlement;
2. a hidden nation of the dead;
3. empires and industrialized undeath;
4. gods and the machinery of the afterlife;
5. the definition of death itself.

## Narrative invariants

- No single ending is declared morally correct.
- The player's methods matter as much as the outcomes.
- Every major route remains grounded in named companions and promises.
- Vael, the Earth-born protagonist, and the Second Voice remain distinct identity questions until late Act IV.
- Factions contain sincere, corrupt, pragmatic, and radical members.
- The player cannot save everything. Final power requires choosing what may be lost.
- All story content is human-authored and resolved by deterministic state.

## Act I — The Dead of Dunmire

Scope: personal survival and one region.

The protagonist awakens at Vael's execution, reaches Dunmire, encounters speaking corpses, meets the four core companions, and enters Saint Orra's Descent. They discover that official funerary rites damage souls and that an earlier covenant once protected complete identities.

Act climax: choose how to handle Saint Orra's failing covenant.

Persistent outputs:

- first public identity;
- first necromancy philosophy;
- Dunmire state;
- Seraphine, Morrow, Lys, and Aldren relationship baselines;
- Church/Hollow/Empire awareness;
- first covenant fragment;
- witnesses and rumors;
- preserved, released, enslaved, or destroyed named souls.

## Act II — The Kingdom Without Breath

Scope: political recognition of the dead.

The player enters the Hollow Court, a hidden polity whose citizens range from preserved ancestors to constructed composite persons. A succession crisis exposes contradictions in Morrow's identity and forces the player to define citizenship, consent, memory ownership, and the legal status of undead.

Act climax: determine the Court's government and relationship with living states.

Persistent outputs:

- Hollow Court ruler or council;
- undead personhood doctrine;
- Morrow's accepted identity;
- first territory/army capability;
- access to deep Soulcraft or Grave Dominion;
- alliance, protectorate, exile, destruction, or absorption outcome.

## Act III — The Ashen Engine

Scope: war, industry, and mass consequence.

The Ashen Empire invades border territories using standardized undead labor and military units. Its system prevents famine and reduces living casualties while erasing the identities of condemned people. The player must oppose, reform, capture, or surpass its necromantic infrastructure.

Act climax: the Ashen Engine, a network that can bind millions of residual echoes, is activated, transformed, liberated, or destroyed.

Persistent outputs:

- war outcome;
- mass-undeath policy;
- army and territory scale;
- companion ideological fractures;
- proof that the Gate is consuming identity;
- availability of industrial resources versus decentralized autonomy.

## Act IV — The Hunger of Heaven

Scope: divine institutions and metaphysical truth.

The party reaches the mechanisms surrounding the Luminous Gate. The gods are revealed as both exploiters and wardens: their consumption of memories sustains a barrier against the Nameless Below. Vael helped design a replacement but divided his soul to hide it. The Earth-born protagonist was selected as an external continuity capable of choosing outside the world's divine contracts.

Act climax: enter the Gate, restore Vael, reject him, merge identities, or establish permanent separation; decide whether to expose the truth.

Persistent outputs:

- protagonist identity configuration;
- fate of the Church and gods;
- integrity of the barrier;
- complete covenant clauses;
- which factions reach the final assembly;
- irreversible companion sacrifices or transformations.

## Act V — The Last Covenant

Scope: definition of life, death, and continuity.

The cycle collapses. Spirits remain, births inherit excessive memory, vessels awaken without owners, and the Below manifests through erased names. The player assembles—or conquers—the powers shaped in prior acts and proposes a final covenant.

The ending is not selected from a menu. It is constructed from eligible covenant clauses earned through skills, discoveries, relationships, territory, promises, and sacrifices.

Final questions:

- Who owns a soul?
- Must identity end?
- Who pays to preserve the barrier?
- May the dead vote, inherit, reproduce, or rule?
- Can gods exist without consumption?
- Is forgetting mercy, murder, or both?
- Does the protagonist retain the borrowed body?

## Companion through-lines

- Seraphine: obedience -> doubt -> reform/rejection -> authority or martyrdom.
- Morrow: claimed identity -> contradiction -> constructed self -> ruler, citizen, chorus, or dissolution.
- Lys: feared foresight -> personal agency -> map of possible deaths -> guardian or destroyer of predestination.
- Aldren: duty -> memory erosion -> chosen limits -> person beyond oath or willing final sacrifice.

## Campaign failure philosophy

Act-level failure changes power, allies, territory, and final eligibility but rarely blocks continuation. Hard endings occur only when clearly telegraphed metaphysical conditions are accepted or when all continuity anchors are deliberately exhausted.

## Content scale target

Full release target, subject to production validation:

- 5 acts;
- 8–12 major regions;
- 10–14 factions/subfactions;
- 8 core companions;
- 150–220 substantial quests;
- 60–100 tactical encounters;
- 6 complete disciplines;
- 12–20 ending families with state-driven variants;
- 40–70 hour first campaign;
- high replay value through mutually exclusive institutions, skills, and covenant clauses.
