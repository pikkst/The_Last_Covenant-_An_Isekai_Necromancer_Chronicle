# Act III — The Ashen Engine

## Purpose

Act III tests ideals at scale. Personal necromancy meets an empire that uses death as infrastructure. The player gains army and territory power while confronting the cost of standardization.

## Core conflict

The Ashen Empire claims its system:

- prevents famine;
- replaces dangerous labor;
- reduces living battlefield deaths;
- preserves state stability;
- gives condemned citizens posthumous utility.

Its hidden costs:

- legal erasure of personhood;
- inherited corpse debt;
- memory stripping;
- permanent imperial command keys;
- expansion required to feed the system;
- deliberate creation of disposable populations.

## Regions

- Cinder March;
- City of Furnaces;
- the Corpse Rail;
- Fields of Standing Laborers;
- Imperial Archive of Debts;
- the Ashen Engine complex.

## Major chapters

### III.1 — Border of Useful Dead

The player encounters villages dependent on imperial undead labor. Simply destroying it causes hunger.

### III.2 — War of Command

Enemy command hierarchies challenge the player's undead. Espionage, sabotage, counter-binding, diplomacy, and battle routes diverge.

### III.3 — The Price of Bread

The player must replace or accept systems sustaining civilians. Territory economy begins affecting moral choices.

### III.4 — Archive of Erased Names

Evidence reveals who became legally non-persons and how memories were harvested. Morrow's source identities may be present.

### III.5 — Companion Fracture

Companions respond to mass decisions. High affection cannot automatically prevent departure or opposition.

### III.6 — The Ashen Engine

The network can be captured, decentralized, liberated, rewritten, sealed, or annihilated. Each option has immediate and delayed casualties.

## Strategic systems

- armies and formations;
- supply and maintenance;
- territory needs;
- civilian stability;
- command-network warfare;
- officers with agency;
- multi-front campaign choices;
- delegating authority.

The game remains text-first. Strategy uses decisions, policies, assignments, reports, and authored crises rather than real-time map micromanagement.

## Antagonist: Legate Cassian Rook

Rook believes moral purity is a luxury purchased by invisible suffering. He will accept reforms that demonstrably preserve food and defense, but he refuses collapse without replacement.

Possible outcomes:

- executed;
- converted into a bound advisor;
- allied reformer;
- independent rival;
- voluntary final servant;
- emperor;
- exposed and rejected by his own forces.

## Act III climax outputs
