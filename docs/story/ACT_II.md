# Act II — The Kingdom Without Breath

## Purpose

Act II turns necromancy from a private practice into a political question. The player must decide whether the dead can form a society and whether continuity, memory, consent, or recognition defines citizenship.

## Entry states

Act II adapts to Act I:

- Dunmire saved, occupied, abandoned, transformed, or destroyed;
- Hollow Court friendly, wary, hostile, or unaware;
- Morrow recruited, rejected, imprisoned, altered, or destroyed;
- Seraphine loyal to Church, doubtful, exiled, or allied;
- first covenant handled through mercy, sovereignty, tyranny, compromise, or failure.

No valid Act I result blocks Act II. Missing characters receive authored replacement routes with different costs.

## Political crisis

The Hollow Court's ancient sovereign has not spoken for decades. Five claimants compete:

1. Morrow, claiming King Morcant's continuity;
2. the Chorus, a collective of consenting spirits;
3. Lady Veyra, an intact aristocratic revenant;
4. Marshal Keth, defender demanding wartime rule;
5. the Unnamed Assembly, citizens who surrendered former identities.

Each represents a different definition of personhood.

## Major chapters

### II.1 — Invitation or Abduction

The player is summoned, lured, arrested, or follows a stolen soul into Court territory. They learn the Court is real and divided.

### II.2 — The Census of Names

A ritual census is failing. Citizens disappear when their names are challenged. The player investigates identity fraud, memory trade, and deliberate erasure.

### II.3 — Five Claims

Meet claimants and complete or sabotage political trials: continuity, service, consent, recognition, and sacrifice.

### II.4 — The Empty Throne

Morrow's memories are proven composite. The player may expose, conceal, repair, divide, or affirm him as a newly constructed person.

### II.5 — Siege Without Sunrise

Church radicals, imperial scouts, or player-created Act I enemies attack. The crisis tests the proposed government.

### II.6 — A Constitution for the Dead

The player establishes laws or imposes rule. Clauses become persistent world mechanics.

## Constitutional dimensions

- personhood requirement;
- right to refuse binding;
- ownership of remains;
- memory-copy rights;
- status of echoes;
- living residency;
- military obligation;
- succession;
- right to final death;
- punishment and rehabilitation.

The result changes quests, army recruitment, resource costs, faction reactions, and final covenant options.

## Companion arcs

- Morrow confronts whether a false history can support a real present.
- Seraphine sees functional undead society and Church crimes, but also genuine predation.
- Lys becomes a political symbol against her wishes.
- Aldren petitions for release from an oath or accepts elected service.

## Act II completion gate

Act II ends when the succession crisis and siege are resolved, Morrow's identity receives a durable outcome, and the player establishes a policy toward undead political agency.
