# Rival Necromancers

## Purpose

Rivals demonstrate alternative necromantic philosophies, react to the player's methods, and create personal long-term opposition without using generative behavior.

## Rival model

- identity and origin;
- philosophy;
- disciplines/build;
- resources;
- sanctuary;
- followers;
- knowledge;
- relationship with player;
- grievances/debts;
- goals and plans;
- boundaries;
- known player methods;
- adaptation flags;
- possible transformations;
- defeat/recruitment outcomes.

## Rival candidates

### The Vessel Collector

Believes bodies are abandoned architecture. Collects vessels the player leaves behind and may animate a former protagonist body.

### The Kindly Eraser

Ends suffering by removing painful identity. Genuinely reduces unrest while destroying continuity.

### The Red Anatomist

Fleshweaver offering perfect replacement bodies at the price of ownership and experimentation.

### The Unbound Captain

A former controlled undead who builds a liberation movement and judges the player's treatment of servants.

### Vael's Heir

Claims philosophical or biological inheritance from Vael and rejects the Earthborn occupant.

## Adaptation

Rivals react through authored state rules:

- observe repeated combat tactics;
- acquire counters;
- recruit people harmed by the player;
- exploit broken promises;
- contest legends;
- offer better covenants to undead;
- secure remains after battles;
- imitate or condemn policies.

Adaptation is bounded, telegraphed, and deterministic.

## Former vessel rival

If the protagonist abandons or loses a sufficiently preserved vessel, conditions may allow another identity to occupy it. The former body retains habits, reputation, access, and claims, creating a mirror rival.

This is not guaranteed in every death and must respect confirmed vessel destruction.

## Rival lifecycle

- rumor;
- first indirect consequence;
- encounter;
- ideological test;
- adaptation;
- escalation;
- crisis;
- resolution or continuing coexistence.

## Resolutions

- kill/finally destroy;
- release;
- expose;
- imprison;
- recruit;
- reconcile;
- merge organizations;
- accept rivalry;
- lose and serve;
- empower as an independent endgame claimant.

## Fairness

Rivals know only information they observe or obtain. They cannot counter a build retroactively without setup. Their strength follows campaign resources and difficulty policy.

## Testing

Test knowledge boundaries, missing characters, former vessels, competing plans, repeated tactics, reconciliation, death, Act transitions, and ending claimant eligibility.
