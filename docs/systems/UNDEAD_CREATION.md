# Undead Creation and Lifecycle

## Composition

`Vessel + Animating Identity + Binding + Role + Equipment + Memory Pattern + Anchor`

Each component is explicit and versioned.

## Creation workflow

1. inspect vessel and identity evidence;
2. identify claims and consent;
3. choose animation method;
4. repair/prepare vessel;
5. select spirit, echo, composite, or impulse;
6. define binding/covenant;
7. assign role;
8. provide anchor/equipment;
9. perform ritual;
10. resolve stability and identity;
11. name/recognize result;
12. record witnesses and obligations.

## Vessel properties

- origin;
- species;
- condition;
- decay;
- missing parts;
- embodied memories;
- magical alterations;
- legal/family claims;
- preservation;
- compatibility.

## Identity properties

- Name;
- Memory;
- Will;
- Thread;
- consent;
- goals;
- relationships;
- stability;
- previous bindings;
- passage preference.

## Binding models

- direct coercion;
- bounded command;
- voluntary service;
- mutual covenant;
- elected duty;
- role imprint;
- autonomous return;
- echo pattern;
- empty impulse.

## Roles

Examples:

- guardian;
- scout;
- archivist;
- laborer;
- ritual assistant;
- diplomat;
- captain;
- vessel reserve;
- healer;
- infiltrator.

Roles define permissions and learned behavior, not immutable personality.

## Instability

Causes:

- incompatible vessel;
- damaged Name;
- conflicting memories;
- coerced Will;
- weak anchor;
- excessive distance;
- contradictory orders;
- corruption;
- Gate events.

Instability produces authored symptoms and mechanical risks.

## Growth

Named undead can:

- learn;
- gain titles;
- change role;
- form relationships;
- request modification;
- renegotiate service;
- recover memories;
- oppose orders;
- become independent;
- choose final death.

## Maintenance

Costs may be physical repair, Essence, ritual renewal, memory grounding, social recognition, or fulfilling the covenant. Not every undead consumes the same generic resource.

## Destruction and recovery

Separate vessel destruction, identity damage, anchor loss, binding break, and final passage. Recovery depends on preserved continuity evidence.

## UI requirements

The player sees known composition, risks, obligations, autonomy, maintenance, and claims. Hidden identity facts remain discoverable. Irreversible creation steps require contextual confirmation.

## Testing

Use combinatorial validation to prevent invalid component states, orphaned identities, duplicated unique spirits, negative costs, and resurrection after confirmed final destruction.
