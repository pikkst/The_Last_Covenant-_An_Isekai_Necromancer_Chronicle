# Territory, Governance, and Kingdom Systems

## Purpose

Territory is a network of people, needs, institutions, laws, and locations—not passive map coloring.

## Territory state

- owner/controller;
- population by relevant groups;
- stability;
- safety;
- food;
- shelter;
- health;
- labor;
- burial capacity;
- spirit pressure;
- legitimacy;
- infrastructure;
- faction influence;
- active crises;
- laws/policies;
- connections and supply.

## Governance models

- personal rule;
- council;
- constitutional monarchy;
- republic;
- military protectorate;
- religious administration;
- federated sanctuaries;
- distributed covenant network.

Governance changes who decides during absence and how succession works.

## Policy domains

- body/remains rights;
- undead citizenship;
- binding consent;
- final-death right;
- posthumous labor;
- memory copying;
- taxation/resources;
- military service;
- religious freedom;
- refugee status;
- living/undead inheritance;
- emergency authority.

## Needs and crises

Territories generate authored crises from state thresholds and campaign events. Examples: famine, spirit crowding, plague, burial dispute, captain rebellion, refugee influx, divine failure, memory contamination.

## Delegation

Appoint governors, councils, companions, undead captains, or institutions. Delegates act through deterministic policies based on ideology, competence, loyalty, law, and information.

## Legitimacy

Legitimacy differs among populations. Fear, legal recognition, material success, tradition, election, divine sanction, and covenant may support rule.

## Expansion

Acquire territory by invitation, treaty, inheritance, liberation, purchase, occupation, conquest, or metaphysical sanctuary. Method remains in history.

## Failure and loss

Territory can revolt, defect, be occupied, evacuate, fragment, or become uninhabitable. Loss creates continuation content rather than deleting all investment without response.

## Player organization

The protagonist's faction has:

- charter;
- name and symbol;
- capital/sanctuary;
- leadership;
- citizenship;
- doctrine;
- laws;
- diplomacy;
- military;
- succession;
- amendment rules.

## UI

Provide dashboard, reports, policy decisions, map/list, alerts, forecasts with uncertainty, and delegate explanations. Avoid spreadsheet overload in the main narrative.
