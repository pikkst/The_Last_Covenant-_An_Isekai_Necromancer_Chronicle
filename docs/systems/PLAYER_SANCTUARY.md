# Player Sanctuary

## Purpose

The Sanctuary is the player's emotional and strategic home. It preserves named characters, displays consequences, supports preparation, and eventually becomes an institution.

## Forms

The initial Sanctuary form depends on campaign choices:

- abandoned crypt;
- moving funerary caravan;
- restored chapel;
- hidden memory garden;
- underground Hollow district;
- bone-built mobile fortress.

Forms can transform or relocate. Choice affects capabilities, exposure, inhabitants, and symbolism.

## State

- name and public identity;
- location/form;
- capacity;
- security;
- secrecy;
- living population;
- undead population;
- spirit population;
- facilities;
- laws;
- assigned leaders;
- resources;
- morale;
- unresolved disputes;
- faction claims;
- attack/evacuation readiness.

## Facilities

- mortuary and preservation room;
- memory archive;
- spirit sanctuary;
- bone workshop;
- ritual chamber;
- living quarters;
- council hall;
- infirmary;
- training ground;
- command relay;
- reliquary;
- vessel vault;
- garden/kitchen;
- defensive wards.

Facilities unlock options rather than only passive percentages.

## Inhabitants

Named undead, companions, teachers, refugees, families, workers, petitioners, prisoners, and visitors have needs and relationships. The Sanctuary must not reduce people to generic population counters.

## Governance

Early decisions are personal. Later the player creates rules, appoints responsibility, hears disputes, and establishes succession. Sanctuary law becomes the prototype for territory and final covenant policy.

## Events

Events arise from population, facilities, secrecy, faction pressure, promises, laws, and time:

- spirit disputes;
- family claims;
- forbidden experiment;
- refugee arrival;
- disease;
- betrayal;
- religious visit;
- attack;
- captain election;
- request for final death;
- memory archive contradiction.

## Attack and loss

The Sanctuary may be infiltrated, besieged, occupied, evacuated, divided, or destroyed. Important inhabitants receive explicit outcome resolution and possible escape routes.

## Customization

Name, emblem, public doctrine, architecture theme, memorials, displayed relics, and key rooms reflect choices. Cosmetic choices never conceal required accessibility information.

## UX

Provide an explorable text hub, concise overview, petitions, facilities, inhabitants, policies, and preparation. Avoid mandatory repeated visits for trivial collection.

## Testing

Test capacity, facility prerequisites, inhabitant continuity, relocation, attack, delegation, incompatible laws, save migration, and all valid Sanctuary forms.
