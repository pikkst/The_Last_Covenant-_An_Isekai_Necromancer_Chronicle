# Crafting, Rituals, and Relics

## Goals

Crafting supports build identity, preparation, investigation, and narrative choices. It is not a repetitive material grind.

## Categories

- body preservation/repair;
- bone tools and weapons;
- spirit anchors;
- ritual diagrams;
- alchemical components;
- command relays;
- vessels;
- relic restoration;
- covenant documents;
- sanctuary structures.

## Recipe model

- stable ID;
- discovery source;
- station/site;
- skills/tools;
- components;
- substitutions;
- time;
- risks;
- deterministic outputs;
- quality factors;
- signatures;
- ethical/legal tags;
- failure products.

## Components

Components carry provenance and claims. Human remains are never reduced to a neutral generic icon without origin metadata where relevant.

## Ritual lifecycle

- discover;
- prepare;
- assign participants;
- select components/substitutions;
- choose timing/site;
- begin;
- handle interruptions;
- resolve;
- clean up/contain;
- record consequences.

## Quality

Quality is explainable and bounded. It affects durability, stability, capacity, signature, or side effects, not arbitrary rarity colors.

## Relics

Unique relics have:

- history;
- identity;
- current condition;
- attunement;
- memory;
- factions/claimants;
- transformations;
- destruction consequence.

Relics can change through use and narrative decisions.

## Stations

Examples: mortuary table, bone forge, memory basin, covenant circle, field kit, sanctuary workshop. Territory investment can improve availability.

## Automation

Later progression allows trusted assistants and templates to repeat safe known work. Automation consumes resources and respects policy; it cannot silently use protected remains.

## Balance

Crafting provides alternate solutions and preparation advantages but does not require collecting hundreds of trivial items. Important recipes have multiple acquisition paths.
