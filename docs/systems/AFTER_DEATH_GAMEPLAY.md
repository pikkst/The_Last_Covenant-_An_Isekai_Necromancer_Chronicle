# After-Death Gameplay

## Purpose

Protagonist death is a playable transition. It creates cost, discovery, urgency, and identity decisions rather than immediately becoming a load screen.

## Entry conditions

After-death play depends on:

- manner of death;
- Thread condition;
- Name stability;
- prepared anchors/vessels;
- active pacts;
- skills;
- companion actions;
- location;
- Gate/world state;
- difficulty rules.

Some forms of final destruction remain possible when clearly telegraphed.

## Spirit state

While disembodied, the protagonist may have:

- limited movement;
- altered perception;
- spirit communication;
- memory exposure;
- possession attempts;
- anchor interaction;
- reduced access to physical actions;
- declining stability;
- risk of detection/consumption;
- identity interference from Vael/Second Voice.

## After-death objectives

- reach an anchor;
- protect or recover remains;
- contact companions;
- negotiate with a spirit;
- enter a prepared vessel;
- possess a compatible unclaimed vessel;
- complete a covenant clause;
- sacrifice a memory;
- accept help and debt;
- investigate a hidden death-layer;
- choose final passage.

## Time and pressure

After-death state uses game time and explicit stability pressure, not real-world timers. Consequences are telegraphed.

## Vessel return

A candidate vessel requires:

- availability;
- compatibility;
- legal/ethical claims;
- preparation;
- physical condition;
- identity vacancy or negotiated coexistence;
- skill/pact;
- cost.

The server validates all conditions.

## Companion involvement

Companions may preserve the body, retrieve an anchor, perform a ritual, dispute possession, offer sacrifice, exploit vulnerability, or carry on without the protagonist.

## Memory cost

Death may scar, lock, distort, or require sacrificing a specific memory. Costs are recorded and affect identity, skills, relationships, and Chronicle entries.

## Hidden layer

Death perception can reveal Threads, spirit trails, divine extraction, erased identities, and old promises. It must not become a consequence-free method for obtaining every secret.

## Failure outcomes

- return with scar;
- return in altered vessel;
- debt/pact;
- memory loss;
- delayed return after world changes;
- companion-controlled rescue;
- spirit imprisonment;
- successor character;
- final ending.

## Save and recovery

Autosave at stable after-death boundaries. Preserve a pre-death checkpoint while preventing duplication exploits. Restoring a checkpoint is a player recovery feature, not canonical time travel unless explicitly framed.

## Testing

Test every death layer, no-anchor states, simultaneous rescue, invalid vessels, identity conflict, repeated death, final destruction, checkpoint abuse, migration, and all difficulty profiles.
