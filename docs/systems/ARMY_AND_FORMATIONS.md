# Army, Command, and Formations

## Purpose

Army play expands personal necromancy without becoming an unrelated strategy game. Every formation is connected to captains, laws, supply, identity, and player-created institutions.

## Scale tiers

- party: 1–8 active participants;
- unit: 5–30;
- cohort: multiple units;
- legion: multiple cohorts;
- theater: several fronts and territories.

The engine aggregates higher tiers and zooms into decisive authored situations.

## Unit model

- unit ID/name;
- members/template composition;
- captain;
- doctrine;
- role;
- readiness;
- cohesion;
- morale;
- autonomy;
- supply/maintenance;
- location;
- casualties/damage;
- memory and reputation.

Named members remain individually tracked when narratively important.

## Command hierarchy

Protagonist -> high commanders -> captains -> units.

Delegation reduces control load but introduces interpretation, delay, loyalty, ideology, and initiative.

## Orders

- defend;
- patrol;
- escort;
- scout;
- seize;
- delay;
- withdraw;
- rescue;
- suppress;
- negotiate;
- preserve remains;
- avoid living casualties;
- destroy spirits;
- hold ritual site.

Orders include priorities, constraints, authority, duration, and fallback.

## Formations

Examples:

- Bone Wall;
- Silent March;
- Lantern Escort;
- Grave Wedge;
- Memory Relay;
- Hollow Circle;
- Vessel Screen.

Formation effectiveness depends on composition, terrain, training, captain, and counterplay.

## Logistics

Armies need:

- repair/material;
- anchors;
- command relays;
- transport;
- information;
- political permission;
- replacement policy.

Some undead do not need food or rest, but this never means zero logistical cost.

## Casualties

Track destroyed vessels, damaged identities, missing units, captured anchors, broken covenants, and civilian harm. Casualty reports name important losses.

## Agency and refusal

Voluntary forces can refuse illegal or covenant-breaking orders. Coerced forces may rebel, sabotage, or collapse when control weakens.

## Strategic resolution

Campaign operations resolve in phases:

1. intelligence;
2. plan and assignments;
3. opponent response;
4. fronts;
5. decisive scene;
6. casualties and territory;
7. political aftermath.

## Anti-micromanagement

Use policies, doctrines, templates, delegation, and reports. The player handles exceptional decisions rather than repeating maintenance clicks.

## Balance

No army should erase companion-scale play. High power creates exposure, governance, logistics, and moral consequences.
