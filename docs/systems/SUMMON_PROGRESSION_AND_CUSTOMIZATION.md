# Summon Progression and Customization

## Purpose

Every persistent summon can become a distinct build, companion, officer, specialist, or evolving creature. Summons are not disposable copies distinguished only by level.

“Summon” is the player-facing umbrella term for controlled, allied, contracted, or created undead and necromantic entities. Canonical records still distinguish bodies, spirits, echoes, constructs, and autonomous persons.

## Summon identity

A persistent summon contains:

- stable entity ID;
- chosen and former names;
- vessel/body;
- animating identity;
- binding or covenant;
- role;
- level and progression history;
- base traits;
- learned skills;
- equipment/loadout;
- memories;
- titles;
- injuries and modifications;
- autonomy;
- loyalty and relationships;
- needs and maintenance;
- fusion/evolution history;
- cosmetic presentation;
- legal and ethical claims.

## Progression sources

Summons grow through:

- participating in encounters;
- completing assigned duties;
- surviving important events;
- training with the protagonist or another summon;
- recovering memories;
- receiving a title;
- vessel repair or modification;
- equipment attunement;
- ritual evolution;
- fusion;
- independent personal decisions.

Routine grinding must not be the only or optimal growth route.

## Experience and mastery

Progression uses two related concepts:

- **Growth:** general development gained from meaningful participation;
- **Mastery:** practice and narrative qualification for a specific role or skill family.

A summon cannot learn every skill merely by accumulating generic experience.

## Summon archetypes

Initial roles may include:

- Vanguard;

Roles guide available branches but do not permanently lock identity.

## Individual skill trees

Every summon has access to:

1. a universal undead foundation;
2. vessel-specific skills;
3. identity/memory skills;
4. role skills;
5. equipment-granted skills;
6. relationship or covenant skills;
7. evolution/fusion branches;
8. unique title or story nodes.

## Skill acquisition

A summon receives a skill point or unlock opportunity, but skills may require:

- compatible vessel;
- role mastery;
- recovered memory;
- equipment;
- trainer;
- title;
- autonomy level;
- covenant permission;
- fusion component;
- relevant story choice.

The player proposes development. Autonomous summons may accept, reject, or request an alternative.

## Stat model

Candidate summon attributes:

- Structure;
- Force;
- Agility;
- Perception;
- Will;
- Memory;
- Spirit Stability;
- Command Affinity;
- Autonomy;
- Vessel Compatibility.

Derived values are explainable and use bounded modifier stacking.

## Orders and behavior profile

Customization includes behavior, not only combat power:

- preferred range;
- target priorities;
- protection target;
- retreat threshold;
- corpse preservation policy;
- lethal/nonlethal preference;
- spirit-damage restrictions;
- ability-use conditions;
- initiative permissions;
- emergency autonomy.

Orders are constrained policies. They do not become executable scripts.

## Visual customization

Depending on vessel:

- name/title;
- armor appearance;
- bone material/treatment;
- cloth and faction colors;
- engraved covenant marks;
- eye/soul-light;
- weapon display;
- repaired versus battle-worn presentation;
- silhouette/evolution form;
- banner and captain insignia.

Visual options must remain readable and cannot hide gameplay-critical equipment.

## Rarity

Do not assign people a casino-style rarity. Items and body materials may have availability grades, but a summon’s value comes from composition, history, skills, relationships, and build.

## Death and inheritance

On vessel destruction:

- equipment may drop, break, remain bound, or be recovered;
- identity may survive through an anchor;
- skills tied to body may be lost until a compatible vessel is found;
- memories and titles remain if continuity survives;
- another summon may inherit equipment, role, or unfinished duty.

## UX

The Summon Sheet provides:

- identity and known history;
- body and spirit condition;
- attributes and modifiers;
- skill graph plus accessible list;
- equipment;
- behavior profile;
- autonomy/covenant;
- maintenance;
- relationships;
- evolution/fusion eligibility;
- warnings and claims.

## Balance

- no one universal summon build;
- specialists outperform generic units in their domain;
- equipment has tradeoffs;
- high autonomy provides initiative but not guaranteed obedience;
- fusion/evolution creates cost and identity consequences;
- early named summons remain viable through investment;
- army units use templates, while named summons retain full builds.

## Testing

Test progression determinism, skill prerequisites, equipment interactions, behavior policies, autonomy refusal, vessel replacement, destruction/recovery, save migration, and accessible UI projections.
