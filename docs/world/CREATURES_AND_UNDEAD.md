# Creatures and Undead Bestiary

## Classification principles

Creatures are classified by observable properties, not moral alignment. Undead require separate identity and animation classification.

## Undead dimensions

- vessel type and condition;
- animating source;
- Name integrity;
- Memory integrity;
- Will;
- binding;
- autonomy;
- maintenance;
- damage type;
- passage status.

## Core undead categories

### Impulse-animated

A body driven by simple necromantic force. No evidence of original personhood, but emerging behavior must be monitored.

### Echo-bound

Animated by residual habits or memories. May develop beyond initial limits.

### Returned

Original spirit intentionally joined to a compatible vessel.

### Bound

A spirit constrained by coercive command. Strength and instability increase with resistance.

### Covenant dead

Service defined through explicit agreement with scope and revocation rules.

### Revenant

Self-sustaining dead driven by Name, Will, and unresolved purpose.

### Composite

Identity assembled from multiple memories, names, or spirits.

### Hollow

Persistent dead whose physical vessel is absent or replaceable.

### Divine remnant

Identity fragment produced by damaged miracles or dead gods.

### Nameless manifestation

Phenomenon created from erased identity. It may imitate people without stable Name.

## Bestiary entry requirements

Each entry defines:

- stable ID;
- known names;
- origin;
- anatomy/metaphysics;
- behavior;
- habitat;
- needs;
- communication;
- combat policy;
- vulnerabilities/resistances;
- corpse/spirit result;
- ethical concerns;
- faction use;
- codex discovery stages;
- content warnings;
- deterministic encounter hooks.

## Enemy behavior

Behavior uses deterministic policies driven by perception, goals, morale, injuries, relationships, and environment. Creatures do not gain knowledge they could not observe.

## Taming, recruiting, and binding

Not every creature can or should be recruited. Options may include communication, negotiated service, sanctuary, training, ritual binding, habitat restoration, or destruction.

## Boss design

Bosses are narrative systems, not inflated health bars. Each has changing objectives, environmental rules, social or metaphysical leverage, multiple resolution routes, and persistent consequences.

## Content target

Full release targets enough variety that each region has a distinct ecological and metaphysical identity. Variants must change behavior or context, not only color and statistics.
