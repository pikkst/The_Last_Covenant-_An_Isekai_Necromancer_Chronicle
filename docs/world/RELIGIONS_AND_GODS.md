# Religions, Gods, and Funerary Institutions

## Purpose

Religion in Avarra provides community, healing, ethics, power, hierarchy, exploitation, and sincere meaning. The setting does not equate faith with ignorance or institutions with a single motive.

## Radiant tradition

The largest religious network centers on the Luminous Gate and promises safe passage.

### Public teachings

- every life has a sacred Name;
- the dead must not be owned;
- grief must release rather than imprison;
- Light returns memory to the world;
- uncontrolled undeath endangers everyone.

### Hidden system

Senior Keepers know that passage strips identity to sustain divine domains and the barrier. Most clergy do not know.

### Orders

- Lanterns: healers, funeral guides, local welfare.
- Final Dawn: investigators and combatants against dangerous magic.
- Keepers of the Gate: metaphysical administrators.
- Choir of Witness: preserves testimony and doctrine.
- Ashen Penitents: controversial posthumous-service advocates.
- Reformist Choir: seeks transparent consent and alternatives.

## Saint Orra

A historical mediator later canonized. Orra proved a voluntary local covenant could preserve names while supporting the barrier. Different groups suppress or reinterpret this legacy.

## Regional traditions

### Ancestor Houses

Families maintain recognized relations with a limited number of dead. They oppose state ownership but may pressure ancestors to remain.

### Shepherd paths

Independent guides help spirits resolve attachments and pass outside official extraction. Internal divisions concern permanent preservation.

### Hollow civil rites

Undead recognition ceremonies establish current Name, rights, witnesses, and consent.

### Imperial doctrine

The Ashen Empire treats posthumous service as civic continuation. Legal personhood ends selectively, making exploitation administratively invisible.

## Gods

Candidate major domains:

- Aurel, keeper of ordered passage;
- Nemea, memory and testimony;
- Harrow, endings and necessary severance;
- Velis, continuity and oath;
- the Many-Warmed Hearth, communal ancestor presence.

Each deity requires:

- origin claims;
- actual known history;
- domain;
- worship practices;
- miracles;
- institution;
- consumed-memory dependence;
- internal disagreement;
- possible Act IV/V fate.

## Faith mechanics

Faith is not one score. Track:

- doctrine knowledge;
- trust in institution;
- relationship with a god/order;
- vows;
- witnessed miracles;
- public identity;
- heresy status;
- personal belief where player chooses to express it.

## Funerals

Funeral variation creates meaningful gameplay:

- Gate rite;
- Shepherd release;
- ancestor preservation;
- cremation;
- name stone;
- sky exposure;
- imperial collection;
- Hollow recognition.

Bodies and souls may have conflicting claims among deceased wishes, family, law, religion, and public safety.

## Narrative rules

- Include sincere believers on opposing sides.
- Do not use modern real-world religions as direct villains.
- Let institutions perform visible good.
- Separate theological claim, character belief, and canonical observation.
- Major reforms require institutions and practices, not only defeating leaders.
