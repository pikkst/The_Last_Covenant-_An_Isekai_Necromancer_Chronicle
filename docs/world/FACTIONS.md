# Faction Bible

## Faction-state model

Each faction tracks:

- trust;
- fear;
- respect;
- debt;
- ideological alignment;
- awareness;
- hostility;
- leadership;
- resources;
- territory;
- internal stability;
- known player methods.

Relationships are multidimensional. High fear can create obedience without loyalty.

## Radiant Church

Goal: preserve safe passage and social order.

Assets: clergy, healing, records, legitimacy, wards, inquisitors.

Fault lines: secrecy, reform, divine dependence, local autonomy.

Potential futures: reformed, schismatic, collapsed, militant, secularized, player-dominated, decentralized.

## Hollow Court

Goal: secure continuity, recognition, and territory for the dead.

Assets: accumulated expertise, hidden vaults, tireless defenders, memory archives.

Fault lines: identity standards, monarchy, living relations, right to dissolution.

Potential futures: republic, monarchy, protectorate, fragmented vaults, empire, sanctuary, destruction.

## Ashen Empire

Goal: stability and expansion through standardized posthumous utility.

Assets: logistics, corpse rail, census, army, industry, command infrastructure.

Fault lines: personhood, debt, civilian dependency, military rule, imperial succession.

Potential futures: reform, federation, conquest, occupation, collapse, necrocracy.

## Shepherds

Goal: preserve choice and guide damaged spirits.

Assets: local trust, hidden routes, funerary knowledge, sanctuaries.

Fault lines: permanent undeath, intervention, political neutrality.

Potential futures: global passage network, persecuted underground, Church integration, radical final-death movement.

## Free Cities of the Veil

Goal: trade and autonomy amid larger powers.

Assets: money, translators, mixed law, information, mercenaries.

Fault lines: profit from memory trade, refugee policy, neutrality.

## Keepers of the Gate

Goal: preserve the barrier at any cost.

Assets: secret history, divine contracts, Gate access, elite ritualists.

Fault lines: necessity versus privilege; who deserves truth.

## Nameless movements

Groups formed around erased identities. Some seek restoration, some collective identity, some destruction of Names.

## Player-created faction

At sufficient territory scale, the protagonist's organization gains:

- name and emblem;
- charter;
- citizenship policy;
- necromancy doctrine;
- leadership model;
- succession;
- military obligations;
- relationship to living communities;
- resource policy;
- final covenant claim.

The organization reflects actual policies rather than a cosmetic selection.

## Internal politics

Factions have leaders and blocs. Reputation with a faction does not guarantee every member agrees. Leadership changes update policies and available content.

## Diplomacy

Supported actions:

- negotiate;
- recognize;
- ally;
- guarantee;
- threaten;
- infiltrate;
- exchange prisoners/remains;
- share knowledge;
- establish passage/burial treaties;
- declare war;
- demand reparations;
- merge institutions.

## Destruction and continuity

No major faction disappears from the narrative merely because its headquarters falls. Survivors, diaspora, ideology, records, and enemies persist through explicit successor states.
