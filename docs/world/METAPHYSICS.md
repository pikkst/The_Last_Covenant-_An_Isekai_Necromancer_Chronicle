# Metaphysics of Life, Death, and Identity

## Design purpose

Metaphysics must be consistent enough for mechanics and flexible enough for philosophical disagreement. Characters may interpret evidence differently; the engine still needs explicit canonical properties.

## Components of a person

### Vessel

Physical body, including embodied habits and residual impressions.

### Breath

Living animation connecting a vessel to ongoing biological life.

### Name

Continuity recognized by self and others. Not merely a spoken label.

### Memory

Experiences and learned patterns. Memory can fragment, copy, fade, or be forged.

### Will

Capacity to choose and resist.

### Thread

Connection between a person, time, world, and passage beyond death.

A complete living person is an active relationship among these components, not a pile of parts.

## Death

Ordinary death separates Breath, Vessel, and Thread. Name, Memory, and Will transition through the passage process. The Luminous Gate currently strips part of Memory and weakens Name.

## Spirits

A spirit retains sufficient Name/Memory/Will without biological Breath. Stability depends on anchors, recognition, unresolved purpose, environment, and metaphysical damage.

## Echoes

Echoes are residual patterns without proven complete Will. They may answer, repeat, adapt within bounds, or develop. The moment an echo becomes a person is intentionally contested and mechanically observable only through partial indicators.

## Undeath

Undeath joins an animating identity or impulse to a Vessel without ordinary Breath.

Possible arrangements:

- original spirit in original body;
- original spirit in different body;
- different spirit in a body;
- echo animation;
- composite identity;
- empty command impulse;
- divine or Below-driven occupation.

## Consent

Consent has scope, knowledge, duration, revocation rules, and coercion context. A past agreement is not automatically unlimited. Binding systems record consent claims and evidence rather than a single moral boolean.

## Memory transfer

Copying memory does not automatically transfer Name or Will. Multiple persons can originate from one memory set. Destroying the source after copying is not treated as safe continuity by default.

## Soul damage

Damage may affect:

- memory access;
- name stability;
- will;
- Thread;
- vessel compatibility;
- perception;
- ability to pass.

Repair can change identity and therefore has ethical as well as mechanical consequences.

## The Luminous Gate

The Gate is a world-scale passage and barrier mechanism. It:

- routes Threads;
- separates incompatible forces;
- extracts memory energy;
- supports divine domains;
- reinforces the barrier against the Below;
- returns fragments to future life.

It is neither a simple location nor a single machine.

## Gods

Gods are persistent identity structures supported by worship, contracts, domains, and consumed memory. Some predate the current Gate; others emerged from it. Gods can change, divide, die, or become institutions.

## The Nameless Below

The Below is what the identity system excludes. Interpretations include:

- pre-personal consciousness;
- collective erased remainder;
- entropy of identity;
- hostile intelligence;
- necessary space allowing distinction.

The game preserves ambiguity while defining observable effects.

## Resurrection constraints

True restoration requires enough continuity across Name, Memory, Will, Thread, and compatible Vessel. Missing components can be reconstructed, but the result may be a successor rather than the same person.

## Isekai transfer

The protagonist comes from outside Avarra's contract network. Their Name and Will attach to Vael's Vessel through a prepared continuity vacancy. This grants unusual freedom from divine clauses but creates identity instability.

## Mechanical requirements

Every corpse/spirit/undead record distinguishes these layers. Skills state which layer they affect. Dialogue never assumes that a moving corpse equals the deceased person without evidence.
