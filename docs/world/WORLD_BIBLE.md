# World Bible

## World name

Working name: **Avarra**.

Avarra is a late-medieval dark-fantasy world shaped by centuries of managed death. Most people know miracles, funeral rites, restless spirits, and forbidden necromancy as facts, but not the machinery behind them.

## Geographic structure

### Vhal March

Act I borderland containing Dunmire, the Grieving Wood, and Saint Orra's Descent. Agriculture, pilgrimage, gravekeeping, and refugee movement define the region.

### The Hollow Reaches

Act II subterranean and twilight territories occupied by persistent dead. Architecture prioritizes memory, acoustics, ritual recognition, and bodies that do not breathe.

### Ashen Empire

Act III continental power with furnace cities, corpse rail, centralized records, standardized necromancy, and expansionist logistics.

### Radiant Principalities

Church-aligned states varying from sincere welfare kingdoms to inquisitorial regimes.

### Free Cities of the Veil

Trade cities where funerary traditions and controlled spirit practice coexist uneasily.

### Northern Ossuary

Ancient battlefield region where layers of remains create unstable history zones.

### Pilgrim Stair

Highland route toward divine thresholds and the Luminous Gate.

## Historical eras

1. **Age Before Names** — mythic period when life and death were not fully divided.
2. **Age of First Breath** — mortality and identity become distinct.
3. **War of Departing Gods** — early gods establish the barrier.
4. **First Covenant Era** — mortals accept funerary systems in exchange for protection.
5. **Age of Lanterns** — Radiant Church expands healing and passage rites.
6. **Necromancer Schisms** — practitioners discover identity extraction.
7. **Ashen Expansion** — industrial necromancy becomes imperial infrastructure.
8. **Current Fracture** — Gate instability causes campaign events.

Exact dates use a campaign calendar defined during content implementation.

## Cultures

Each culture requires:

- naming conventions;
- family and inheritance;
- treatment of bodies;
- funerary rites;
- attitude toward preserved memory;
- social class;
- food, clothing, architecture, music;
- laws around magic and undeath;
- internal diversity and dissent.

No culture is a single moral stereotype.

## Languages

Use a translation abstraction even when all release text is English. In-world languages affect literacy and information, not player accessibility. Important content always has a legitimate translation route.

Candidate language families:

- Vhalic;
- High Radiant;
- Imperial Ash;
- Vault Cant;
- Old Orrian;
- Name-sign used by spirits.

## Technology

Baseline:

- late-medieval craft and agriculture;
- printing limited but expanding;
- alchemy and ritual instrumentation;
- imperial corpse-powered logistics;
- divine healing with political control;
- no modern firearms in initial release unless introduced by explicit later design.

The Earth-born protagonist's knowledge is origin-gated and constrained by available materials, institutions, and actual expertise.

## Economy

Coin exists, but campaign power also depends on:

- land and harvest;
- burial rights;
- ritual materials;
- names and records;
- favors/debts;
- transport;
- skilled living labor;
- preserved undead expertise;
- divine authorization.

## Law and personhood

Legal questions vary by region:

- when a body ceases to belong to a person;
- whether descendants can authorize return;
- whether an undead person inherits;
- whether copies share liability;
- whether a spirit can testify;
- whether coerced posthumous service is punishment or slavery;
- whether final death is a right.

## Travel and scale

Travel takes time and causes state changes. Regions are connected by authored route graphs. Distance, control, season, supply, and metaphysical instability modify access.

## Seasons and calendar

A four-season calendar is sufficient initially, with local holy days and funerary observances. World clocks must support quest deadlines without forcing real-world waiting.

## Canon management

World facts are classified:

- public truth;
- common belief;
- regional belief;
- faction doctrine;
- secret operational fact;
- metaphysical truth;
- unresolved interpretation.

Content must reference the correct knowledge layer.
